# /aios-online:research

The technique: serious research doesn't trust a single model. You write the question well, ask it
in two places, and compare.

## Step 1 · The prompt

If the goal isn't clear, ask one thing: what will you use what you find for? Then write the
research prompt in this shape, in English:

- **Goal:** one paragraph on what we want to know, why, and how it will be used.
- **Type:** exploratory (map a territory), comparative (choose between options), method (how to
  do something well), or verification (is this true?).
- **Core questions:** three to seven, each answerable on its own.
- **Sources:** primary sources before commentary; from the last twelve months if the topic moves
  fast; at least two skeptical voices; numbers with a source; flag whatever appears in only one
  source.
- **Answer format:** executive summary; findings per question with sources; patterns; surprises;
  confidence per question (high, medium, low); list of sources with links; open questions.
- **Context:** what the person already knows and the angle that interests them. Take the angle
  from their work in the `my-business` Doc: who buys, who sells, how money is made in that world and
  what moves it.

Show it in the chat and save it as a new Google Doc in their AIOS folder, in `notes` (or, if it is about one of their clients, in that client's folder under AIOS → projects), with the Google Drive connector, and give them the link.

## Step 2 · Two runs

Run the prompt yourself here, with web search, and save your report as a new Google Doc in their AIOS folder, in `notes` (or, if it is about one of their clients, in that client's folder under AIOS → projects), with the Google Drive connector, and give them the link.
Before they take the prompt to another AI, remove client names and any private detail unless they
say it's fine. Then ask the person to paste the same prompt into another AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, whichever
they have at hand) and bring you the answer: pasted here or attached as a file. If there is only
one report, say so and wait; don't triangulate with one.

## Step 3 · Triangulation

With the two reports, three buckets:
- **Signal:** what both found separately. High confidence, even more if they got there through
  different sources.
- **Complement:** what only one brought and doesn't contradict the other. Say which one brought
  it, and flag whatever smells made up: extraordinary claims with no checkable source.
- **Contradiction:** where they oppose each other. This is the real work: say who has the better
  source, or what needs to be verified.

Save a Google Doc `<topic> · triangulated` in AIOS → notes: the answer to each core question with its confidence level, the
surprises, what stays open, and the sources worth reading. Close with what the person should do
with this, in two lines.
