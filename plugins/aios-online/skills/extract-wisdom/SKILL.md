# /aios-online:extract-wisdom

The goal is the feeling of a smart friend who watched the thing and tells you about it over
coffee: the parts that surprised them, what they'd steal, and what it means for you. Not a book
report.

## 1 · Get the content

- **Article or web page:** read it from the web.
- **YouTube or a podcast with a page:** get the transcript from the web (the video page, the
  episode's own transcript or show notes). If you can't get one, say so and ask them to paste it:
  in YouTube, "...more" under the video, then "Show transcript", copy, paste here.
- **An audio file** (a voice memo, a recording): ask for its transcript (Granola, Otter, or the
  phone's own transcription); this app can't transcribe audio files for them.
- **A text file or pasted text:** use it as it is.

If the content is behind a login or a paywall, say so in one line and ask for the text.

## 2 · Read it all, then build the sections

Read the whole thing first. Then decide what is actually in it and make 3 to 7 sections around
that (for example "How she prices", "What they got wrong about AI", "Habits worth copying"), not
the same headings every time. Under each, 3 to 8 bullets. Each bullet is one idea in a full,
plain sentence, with the speaker's exact words in quotes when the phrasing is the point.

Then always three short closing parts:
- **In one sentence:** the core idea.
- **For your work:** two to four lines connecting it to what this person is working on (read
  the `about-me` Doc and the `priorities` Doc). If nothing connects, say so honestly.
- **Worth your time?** Whether the full thing is worth watching or reading, and which minutes or
  part if only some of it is.

Rules: no filler words ("delves into", "highlights the importance of"), no inventing quotes, no
claims the content doesn't make. If something is the speaker's opinion and not a fact, say "she
argues", not "it is".

## 3 · Save it

Save it as a new Google Doc `YYYY-MM-DD <title>` in their AIOS folder, in `notes` (Google Drive
connector), or in AIOS → projects → <project> if it was asked for a project. If the content is a client's own material (their
recording, their document), it always goes in that client's project, never in their shared notes. Show the person the "In one sentence" and "For
your work" parts in the chat, and give them the link to the full notes.
