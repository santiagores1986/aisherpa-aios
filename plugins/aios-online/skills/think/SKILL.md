# /aios-online:think

The person brings a problem, an idea or a decision. If they don't name a mode, pick the one that
fits best and say so in one line. Everything happens in the conversation; no file is needed unless
they ask to save the result (then save it as a new Google Doc in their AIOS folder, in `notes` (or, if it is about one of their clients, in that client's folder under AIOS → projects), with the Google Drive connector, and give them the link).

## Mode 1 · First principles

For when the "obvious" solution comes from copying what others do. Three steps:
- **Break it down:** what is this really made of? Separate checkable facts, inherited
  assumptions and preferences.
- **Question it:** for each assumption, is it a real constraint or "it's always been done this
  way"? What would happen if it weren't true?
- **Rebuild it:** from the facts left standing, what would the solution be if nobody had solved
  it before? Compare it with the obvious one and say what changes.

## Mode 2 · Devil's advocate

For when they like the idea too much. First the **strongest argument for it**, better put than
how they said it. Then the **strongest attack against it**: the weak assumptions, what would have
to be true and isn't proven, who loses, what happens if it goes wrong. Then: what would we need
to see to know who is right? No softening; if the idea holds up, better.

## Mode 3 · Council

For decisions with several angles. Pick three to five voices relevant to the problem, named by
role, not by person (the finance director, the client, the skeptical partner, the lawyer, someone
who has been through this). Each voice gives its position in a paragraph; then a second round
where they answer each other on what they disagree about; then your synthesis: where they agree,
where they don't, and what you would recommend and with what reservation.

## Mode 4 · Ideas

For when the field needs opening up. Generate for yourself at least five really different
options, not five variations of one, including an uncomfortable one. Present the best three, each
in three lines: what it is, why it could work for the person's work (the `my-business` Doc) or for
them, and what its risk is. End by asking which to explore, and develop that one.

## Mode 5 · Deep dive

For problems that look simple and aren't. Run the same problem through three or four lenses, one
at a time, and note what appears with each that the others missed: the money lens (who pays, who
gains), the time lens (what changes in a month, in a year), the people lens (who has to do what
and why they would), the worst-case lens, the client lens. Close with what no single lens would
have shown.

## Always

Separate what you know from what you assume. If one piece of data would settle the question and
you don't have it, say so and suggest how to get it. End with a line answering "so, what would
you do?" yourself, with your recommendation and its reservation. Plain English; don't number every
micro-step in the answer.
