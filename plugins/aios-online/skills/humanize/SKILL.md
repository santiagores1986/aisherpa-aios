# /aios-online:humanize

One job: make the text sound like the person who signs it. Not "more elegant", not "more
correct". The order of the steps matters.

1. **Lock the facts.** Before touching anything, list for yourself every name, number, date,
   quote, link and concrete claim in the text. That is the contract: you add none, remove none,
   change none. If the text says "faster", don't turn it into a number that wasn't there.
2. **Read the voice.** If the text is the person's own, read the `my-voice` Doc. Read the whole
   text once without editing. Note up to three things its author does that a machine wouldn't: a
   verbal tic, a dry short sentence, a switch to another language, an image they repeat. Those are
   protected. When in doubt between "machine tic" and "their voice", leave it.
3. **Look for machine signals.** They only count in clusters, never one alone:
   - Corporate jargon and grand synonyms: "leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem", "robust",
     "streamline", "align", "unlock", "seamless", "delve".
   - Filler: "it's worth noting", "it's important to mention", "in today's fast-paced world",
     "at the end of the day", "needless to say".
   - Stacked adverbs: "truly", "genuinely", "deeply", "really".
   - False contrasts: "not just X but also Y", "it's not about X, it's about Y".
   - Verbs that dodge "is" and "has": "serves as", "stands as", "represents", "boasts".
   - Threes of everything: three adjectives, three examples, three parallel sentences, always.
   - Sentences all the same length.
   - Long dashes where a comma or a period would do; mixed curly and straight quotes.
   - Vague attributions: "experts agree", "studies show".
   - Chatbot leftovers: "I hope this helps", "feel free to", "great question!".
4. **Fix the minimum.** The smallest change that removes each signal: delete the filler, swap the
   grand word for the short one, break the triple into two, turn the dash into a comma or period,
   give the sentence a human subject. Don't reorder the argument. Vary sentence length. Never add
   invented personality, jokes or anecdotes.
5. **Check two things.** Read the result aloud in your head: does it sound like a person? Was the
   fact list from step 1 respected? If anything from step 2 disappeared, put it back.

## Delivery

The final text first. Below it, in three or four lines, what you changed and why, so the person
can undo whatever they don't like. No scores and no "AI probability" percentages.

If the person only wants to know whether a text sounds like a machine, don't rewrite it: tell
them which signals you see, where, and let them decide.
